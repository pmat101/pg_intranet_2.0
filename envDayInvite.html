Respected Sir,

The Ministry of Environment, forests and climate change is regularly doing reforms for industrial development, The EIA Notification 2006 (S.O. 1533(E)) and its subsequent amendments constitute the principal statutory instrument governing Environmental Clearances for industrial and infrastructure projects in India. In the period 2025-2026, this framework has been subject to a series of significant legal and administrative developments that have materially altered the obligations and entitlements of project proponents, regulatory authorities, and State agencies alike. These include:

  1. The EIA Notification 2006, particularly in relation to expansion projects, product mix changes and related compliances, applicability thresholds especially environmental clearance to industrial sheds.
  2. The Office Memorandum dated 29 January 2025 issued by MoEF&CC — addressing provisions relating to validity of Environmental Clearances, extension, transfer, surrender, and the split of EC in cases of partial commissioning;
  3. Progressive amendments to the compliance and monitoring framework — including mandatory environmental audit under the EIA Notification, return filing requirements on the CPCB and PARIVESH portals, and enhanced scrutiny of the adequacy of pollution control systems (ETP, STP, APCS) during compliance reporting;
  4. Developments relating to the PARIVESH 2.0 portal — including changes to the application workflow, upcoming decision-support tools, and automation features at the regulatory end that have altered the process for submission and tracking of EC applications.

Notwithstanding the significance of these developments, there exists a considerable gap between the regulatory intent behind these instruments and their practical understanding at the level of industry. A large number of industrial units — particularly those in the MSME and mid-scale manufacturing segments — are currently operating either in unawareness of these changes or with incomplete understanding of their compliance obligations. This situation carries material legal risk for project proponents and, equally, places an unnecessary burden on the regulatory enforcement apparatus.

The need of the hour is structured, authoritative awareness. Industry requires direct access to regulatory expertise — not through intermediaries, but through a forum where policy makers and senior officials of MoEF&CC and CPCB engage directly with the industrial and consulting community to explain, clarify, and receive the industry's concerns. Such a forum does not presently exist in a structured, recurring format.
